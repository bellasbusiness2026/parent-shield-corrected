## 1. Tex. Fam. Code § 261.307 (HB 730) — Verbal + written notice; civil inadmissibility framing

**Parent-facing summary (softened):**  
After DFPS starts an investigation of a parent or other legal custodian, the department is generally required **at first contact** with the parent or alleged perpetrator to give a **written summary** and a **verbal notification** of listed rights (including counsel, recording interactions/interviews subject to possible later disclosure, refusing home entry or child interview without a court order, withholding certain consents, refusing a drug test, and consulting counsel before agreeing to a voluntary safety plan). The investigator must document the verbal notice, and DFPS must use a verification form and provide a signed copy to the person or their attorney. If the person **does not receive both** the required verbal notification and written summary, information obtained from that person—and other information that would not have been discovered without that information—is **not admissible for use against the person in any civil proceeding**. That is a **civil evidence** consequence under the statute as amended—not a claim that the whole investigation is automatically dismissed, and **not** a universal criminal-evidence bar.

**Official / primary URLs:**  
- Enrolled HB 730 (88R) amending § 261.307: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB00730F.htm  
- Official chapter page (Family Code ch. 261): https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.261.htm  
- High-quality mirror (cites capitol source): https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fam._code_section_261.307

**Confidence:** **High** for notice duties and civil-inadmissibility framing (enrolled HB 730 text). **Medium** for how a particular court applies § 261.307(e) on a given record.  
**Caveats:** Applies (per HB 730 transition text) to investigations of reports made on/after Sept. 1, 2023. Does **not** mean “case automatically thrown out.” Criminal use of statements is a separate analysis.

## 2. Tex. Fam. Code § 261.303 — Refusal of entry; court order in aid of investigation

**Parent-facing summary (softened):**  
A person may not interfere with a DFPS abuse/neglect investigation, but parents are often told they may **refuse home entry** or a child interview **without a court order**—subject to important exceptions. If admission cannot be obtained, a court with family-law jurisdiction **shall order** entrance for interview/examination/investigation when the court has **good cause** to believe the child is in imminent danger of aggravated circumstances (§ 262.2015(b)), **or** **probable cause** that admission is necessary to protect the child from other abuse/neglect. Similar probable-cause standards apply to ordering medical/psych records or exams. Hearings for such orders generally may **not** be ex parte unless there is probable cause that there is no time consistent with the child’s physical health or safety for a full hearing. Orders must include findings on evidence sufficiency; parties may request a copy. Separately, **constitutional exigency**, **warrants**, consent, and other authorities can change what happens at the door—outcomes are **fact-specific**.

**Official / primary URLs:**  
- Enrolled HB 730 amendments to § 261.303: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB00730F.htm  
- Official chapter page: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.261.htm  
- High-quality mirror: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fam._code_section_261.303

**Confidence:** **High** for post-HB 730 statutory structure (good cause / probable cause / ex parte limits). **Medium** for any single “you can always refuse” slogan—exceptions and companion doctrines matter.  
**Caveats:** Soften absolutes about police/DFPS entry. “Order in aid of investigation” language is common in practice materials; confirm the exact court order form used in your county. Do not treat refusal as risk-free in every scenario.

## 3. Tex. Fam. Code § 261.3027 — Alleged-perpetrator interview recording / online posting limits

**Parent-facing summary (softened):**  
Before interviewing an **alleged perpetrator**, DFPS must generally inform the person **orally and in writing** that the person may create an **audio or video** recording of the interview (but not record “in any other manner”), that the person’s recording may be subject to **subpoena** under a court order, and that the person may request a copy of DFPS’s current recording policy. DFPS must document notice and keep a signed written notice in the case file. Separately, an audio or video recording of DFPS’s interview with an alleged perpetrator **may not be posted on an Internet website** in a manner that could identify a party involved in the interview.

**Official / primary URLs:**  
- Enrolled HB 135 (87R) adding § 261.3027: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/billtext/html/HB00135F.HTM  
- Official chapter page: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.261.htm  
- High-quality mirror: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fam._code_section_261.3027  
- Texas Children’s Commission library item (secondary): http://benchbook.texaschildrenscommission.gov/library_item/gov.tx.leg.fam_code/2295

**Confidence:** **High** that § 261.3027 exists with the notice + anti-identifying-web-post rules (enrolled bill + mirrors).  
**Caveats:** Overlaps with, but is not identical to, the broader recording-rights notice in § 261.307. “May not record in any other manner” language is statutory—do not overclaim formats. Not legal advice on whether a particular online post identifies a party.

## 4. Tex. Penal Code § 16.02 — One-party consent (generally) + DFPS Family Code overlay

**Preferred short note (parent-facing):**  
Texas is generally a **one-party-consent** state (**Penal Code § 16.02**). DFPS-related recording rights also appear in **Family Code § 261.307**. **Not legal advice.**

**Slightly fuller summary (still softened):**  
Under Penal Code § 16.02, a person not acting under color of law generally may intercept a wire, oral, or electronic communication if the person is a **party** to the communication, or if **one party** has given prior consent—unless the interception is for the purpose of committing an unlawful act. Oral-communication protection typically turns on a **reasonable expectation of privacy**. Separately, Family Code §§ 261.307 and 261.3027 address recording in DFPS investigations (notice of rights; alleged-perpetrator interview rules; possible disclosure/subpoena; limits on identifying web posts). Interstate calls, hidden cameras, and recording people you are **not** a party to can change the analysis.

**Official / primary URLs:**  
- Official Penal Code ch. 16 page: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.16.htm  
- GetStatute helper: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/getstatute.aspx?Code=PE&Value=16.02  
- High-quality secondary summary (RCFP): https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/texas/  
- Texas State Law Library guide (secondary): https://guides.sll.texas.gov/recording-laws/audio-recording

**Confidence:** **High** for the general one-party participant rule; **medium** for edge cases (expectation of privacy, unlawful purpose, interstate).  
**Caveats:** Not a license to record private conversations you are not part of. DFPS recording notices do not erase later disclosure obligations.

## 5. Tex. Fam. Code § 262.201 — ~14-day adversary hearing (subject to exceptions)

**Parent-facing summary (softened):**  
In suits under §§ 262.101 or 262.105, unless the child has already been returned and any temporary order dissolved, a **full adversary hearing** shall generally be held **not later than the 14th day** after the child was taken into possession by the governmental entity, **unless** the court grants an extension under the statute (including limited postponements tied to attorney appointment or a non-indigent parent’s appearance, commonly described as up to about seven days for good cause, with further agreement-based flexibility in the text). How “day 14” is counted can be affected by related possession provisions. Failure to hold the hearing exactly on day 14 does not automatically end the case in every appellate scenario—practice and case law vary. Ask counsel what deadlines apply in your county.

**Official / primary URLs:**  
- Official chapter page (Family Code ch. 262): https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.262.htm  
- High-quality mirror: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fam._code_section_262.201

- Texas Children’s Commission benchbook (secondary practice summary): http://benchbook.texaschildrenscommission.gov/library_item/gov.texaschildrenscommission.benchbook/42?print=1

**Confidence:** **High** for the “not later than the 14th day” baseline and that statutory extensions exist. **Medium** for local reset practices and consequences of delay.  
**Caveats:** Soften “mandatory 14 days always.” Subject to statutory exceptions and court scheduling realities.

## 6. Tex. Fam. Code § 263.401 — ~1-year dismissal / up to ~180-day extension (subject to exceptions)

**Parent-facing summary (softened):**  
Unless the court has **commenced trial on the merits** or granted an extension under subsection (b) or (b-1), on the **first Monday after the first anniversary** of the temporary order appointing DFPS as temporary managing conservator, the court’s jurisdiction over the DFPS SAPCR seeking termination or DFPS conservatorship is generally **terminated** and the suit is **automatically dismissed** without a court order (with a required notice to parties roughly 60 days before that date). The court may retain the suit for a period **not to exceed 180 days** after that anniversary-based deadline only if it makes required findings (extraordinary circumstances necessitating the child remaining in DFPS temporary managing conservatorship **and** best interest). Further extensions beyond that retained period are tightly limited by the statute. This is a powerful timeline—not a guarantee of any particular outcome for every family.

**Official / primary URLs:**  
- Official chapter page (Family Code ch. 263): https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.263.htm  
- High-quality mirror: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fam._code_section_263.401  
- Justia 2024 text mirror: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/family-code/title-5/subtitle-e/chapter-263/subchapter-e/section-263-401/

**Confidence:** **High** for the one-year automatic-dismissal structure and up-to-180-day extension framework. **Medium** for new-trial/mistrial/remand sub-rules and how “commenced the trial on the merits” is applied.  
**Caveats:** Subject to statutory exceptions and court findings. Do not market as an automatic “case win.”
